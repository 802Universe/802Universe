DISCOVERY - 2.4/5 GHz scanning + the 6 GHz discovery machinery (802.11ax §26.17)8 rows#

MechanismHow It WorksField Notes
Passive scanClient dwells on each channel (~100+ ms) listening for beacons.Mandatory on channels flagged no-active-scan (DFS, unknown regulatory). Slow: full 5 GHz passive sweep can exceed 2-3 s - roam scan budgeting matters.
Active scanClient sends Probe Request (wildcard or directed SSID), waits ProbeTimer (~10-30 ms) per channel for Probe Responses.Fast but leaks client presence (probes reveal MAC - hence randomization). Hidden SSID only answers directed probes.
6 GHz scan rulesLegacy-style blind probing effectively eliminated: 20 MHz-only probes restricted; discovery relies on PSC + in-band FILS Discovery/UPR + out-of-band RNR.Design consequence: clients discover 6 GHz FAST only if 2.4/5 GHz beacons carry correct RNR - misconfigured co-located sets = "phone never sees 6 GHz".
PSC channelsPreferred Scanning Channels: every 4th 20 MHz channel - 5, 21, 37, 53, 69, 85, 101, 117, 133, 149, 165, 181, 197, 213, 229.Clients scan only PSCs on 6 GHz. Best practice: put the 6 GHz BSS primary on a PSC (80 MHz-centered plans do this naturally).
FILS Discovery framesAP broadcasts compact FILS Discovery frames between beacons (max gap 20 TU) on 6 GHz.Alternative to Unsolicited Probe Responses; lower airtime.
Unsolicited Probe Response (UPR)AP broadcasts full Probe Responses every ≤20 TU on 6 GHz.Chattier alternative to FILS Discovery - one or the other is required in-band.
RNR (out-of-band)Reduced Neighbor Report (IE 201) in 2.4/5 GHz beacons advertises the co-located 6 GHz BSS: op class, channel, short-SSID, BSSID, PSD.The primary real-world 6 GHz discovery path. Also carries multiple-BSSID/co-hosted info.
Multiple BSSID (6 GHz)On 6 GHz, co-located SSIDs must share one beacon via Multiple BSSID element (transmitted + nontransmitted profiles).Client bugs parsing MBSSID profiles = "SSID visible on 5 GHz only" tickets.

FULL JOIN TIMELINE - scan → auth → assoc → keys → IP - with typical timings7 rows#

StageFrames / ExchangeTypical Duration & Notes
1. Scan / selectDiscovery as above → client scores candidates (RSSI, band, 11k neighbor data, vendor logic).0.05-3 s depending on method/bands.
2. 802.11 AuthenticationOpen System (2 frames) - or SAE Commit/Confirm (4 frames, WPA3), or FT auth (11r roam).~2-20 ms. SAE adds crypto time (ECC group 19) - visible on low-end IoT CPUs.
3. AssociationAssoc Req (capabilities, RSN/RSNXE, HT/VHT/HE/EHT caps, ML element) → Assoc Resp (AID, status).~2-10 ms. Most capability mismatches surface HERE - see Status codes sheet.
4a. 802.1X/EAP (Enterprise only)EAPOL-Start → EAP Identity → EAP method (PEAP/TLS/TTLS…) via AP to RADIUS → Access-Accept delivers PMK (MSK).50-1000+ ms full auth (TLS handshake + RADIUS RTTs). This is why 11r/OKC/PMK caching exist - skip this on roam.
4b. 4-Way HandshakeM1(ANonce) → M2(SNonce+MIC) → M3(GTK, MIC) → M4. Derives/installs PTK + GTK.~5-20 ms. Failures → Reason 15/17 - see 4-Way sheet.
5. DHCP / IPv6 RS-RADISCOVER/OFFER/REQUEST/ACK (or renew), ND for v6.20-500+ ms; frequently the dominant connect-time cost - always separate L2 vs L3 delay when a customer says "Wi-Fi is slow to connect".
6. First data / portalARP/DNS/captive-portal checks (OS connectivity probes).OS marks network "connected". Total budget: consumer target < 2-3 s cold, < 100 ms warm roam (11r).
