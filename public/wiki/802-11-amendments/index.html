802.11 Amendments

AmendmentFull NameYearCategoryWhat It AddsStatus / Notes
802.11-1997Base Standard1997PHYDefines three original PHYs: FHSS, DSSS, and Infrared. CSMA/CA MAC. 1 and 2 Mbps only.Superseded. Foundation of all later amendments.
802.11cMAC Bridging Procedures1998 (folded into 802.1D)BridgingBridge operation procedures for 802.11 frames. Content merged into IEEE 802.1D bridging standard rather than published as standalone amendment.Historic. Rarely referenced; exists inside 802.1D.
802.11bHigh Rate DSSS1999PHYAdds HR-DSSS PHY (CCK). 5.5 and 11 Mbps. Backward compatible with 802.11 DSSS.Still found in legacy devices. Rolled into 802.11-2007.
802.11aOFDM (5 GHz)1999PHYIntroduces OFDM PHY in 5 GHz. 6-54 Mbps. Not compatible with 2.4 GHz 802.11b.Foundation for all modern OFDM-based standards.
802.11dInternational Roaming2001RegulatoryAllows APs to advertise country codes and permitted channels/power levels. Enables regulatory domain auto-detection.Essential for multi-country device certification. Rolled into 802.11-2007.
802.11gERP-OFDM (2.4 GHz)2003PHYBrings OFDM to 2.4 GHz. 6-54 Mbps. Adds CTS-to-Self and RTS/CTS protection for 802.11b coexistence.Widely deployed era; all 2.4 GHz devices support ERP.
802.11hDFS + TPC (5 GHz)2003RegulatoryMandatory for Europe: Dynamic Frequency Selection (radar avoidance) and Transmit Power Control in 5 GHz.Prerequisite for 5 GHz certification in ETSI regions. DFS now global.
802.11FInter-Access Point Protocol (IAPP)2003 (Trial-Use)RoamingRecommended practice for AP-to-AP handoff context transfer over the DS (roaming context, security context).WITHDRAWN 2006. Replaced in practice by vendor protocols and 802.11r. Capital F = recommended practice, not amendment.
802.11iWPA2 / RSN2004SecurityDefines Robust Security Network (RSN). Introduces CCMP (AES-based) encryption. Replaces WEP/TKIP.Basis of WPA2. All modern certified devices must support 802.11i RSN.
802.11jJapan 4.9/5 GHz2004RegulatoryExtends 802.11a to 4.9 GHz and 5.0 GHz for Japan public safety and licensed use.Japan-specific. Rarely seen outside Japanese public safety networks.
802.11eQoS / WMM2005QoSAdds EDCA (CSMA-CA with differentiated backoff) and HCCA. Defines four access categories: VO/VI/BE/BK.Basis for Wi-Fi Multimedia (WMM) certification. Critical for VoIP and video.
802.11-2007 (TGma)Maintenance Roll-up2007Roll-upConsolidates base + a/b/d/e/g/h/i/j into a single document.Roll-ups renumber clauses - cite the roll-up year when quoting clause numbers.
802.11kRRM2008ManagementRadio Resource Measurement. Enables STAs to report neighbor AP lists, channel load, noise histograms to the AP.Foundation for smart roaming decisions. Works with 802.11r and 802.11v (the 'Triple K').
802.11rFast BSS Transition2008RoamingPre-derives Pairwise Master Keys at neighboring APs for fast re-association without full 802.1X re-auth. Enables <50 ms roam.Essential for enterprise voice/video. Part of 802.11-2012.
802.11y3650-3700 MHz (USA)2008SpectrumLight-licensed operation in US 3.65 GHz band. Adds Contention Based Protocol (CBP) and extended channel switch announcement.Superseded in relevance by CBRS (3.5 GHz, LTE/5G). Historic.
802.11nHT / Wi-Fi 42009PHYMIMO (up to 4×4), 40 MHz channels, A-MPDU/A-MSDU aggregation, STBC, LDPC. Up to 600 Mbps.Transformational: first multi-antenna standard. Still extremely common.
802.11wProtected Mgmt Frames2009SecurityEncrypts and authenticates management frames (deauth, disassoc, action) to prevent deauth attacks.Mandatory in WPA3. Prevents many legacy Wi-Fi attack vectors.
802.11pWAVE / V2X2010V2XDefines Wireless Access in Vehicular Environments (WAVE) for vehicle-to-vehicle (V2V) and V2I comms at 5.9 GHz.Basis of DSRC (Dedicated Short-Range Comms). Being supplemented by C-V2X.
802.11zTunneled Direct Link Setup (TDLS)2010MACSTA-to-STA direct link set up transparently through the AP (setup frames tunneled as data). Direct path can use wider BW/higher MCS than AP link.Used by Miracast and some P2P video use cases. Wireshark: eapol/TDLS action frames, ethertype 0x890D.
802.11sMesh Networking2011MeshDefines Hybrid Wireless Mesh Protocol (HWMP) for self-forming, self-healing mesh networks (802.11 Mesh BSS).Basis of many vendor mesh implementations; also used in Thread/OpenThread inspiration.
802.11uInterworking / Hotspot 2.02011ManagementEnables devices to discover network capabilities before associating. Basis for Passpoint (Hotspot 2.0) and carrier Wi-Fi offload.Critical for seamless cellular offload and venue Wi-Fi. Uses ANQP protocol.
802.11vBSS Transition Mgmt2011RoamingAllows APs to send BSS transition requests to clients, directing them to better APs. Enables load balancing.Completes the roaming trio (k/r/v). Widely implemented in enterprise APs.
802.11adWiGig / 60 GHz2012PHYDefines SC and OFDM PHYs in 60 GHz for multi-Gbps short-range (dock, kiosk, VR). Up to ~7 Gbps.Very short range (~10 m); requires LOS. Basis of WiGig.
802.11aaRobust Audio Video Transport Streaming2012QoSGCR (Groupcast with Retries) for reliable multicast, Stream Classification Service (SCS), intra-AC prioritization, OBSS management for AV.GCR block-ack multicast appears in enterprise APs for multicast video. Basis for parts of Wi-Fi CERTIFIED features.
802.11aePrioritization of Management Frames (QMF)2012QoSQoS Management Frame (QMF) service - allows management frames to be sent using EDCA access categories rather than always highest priority.Niche; some enterprise implementations. Reduces mgmt-frame airtime abuse in dense networks.
802.11-2012 (TGmb)Maintenance Roll-up2012Roll-upAdds k/n/p/r/s/u/v/w/y/z into consolidated standard.-
802.11acVHT / Wi-Fi 52013PHYUp to 8×8 MIMO, 160/80+80 MHz channels, 256-QAM, MU-MIMO DL, beamforming. Up to 6.93 Gbps.Major enterprise/consumer upgrade. 5 GHz only.
802.11afTV White Space2014SpectrumAllows Wi-Fi operation in vacant TV broadcast channels (54-790 MHz) using database-driven spectrum access.'Super Wi-Fi'. Requires geo-location + database query. Limited deployment.
802.11ahHaLow / Sub-1 GHz2016PHYSub-1 GHz Wi-Fi for IoT. Very long range (1 km+), low power. Narrow channels (1-16 MHz). Up to ~347 Mbps.Growing IoT adoption for smart metering, agriculture, industrial.
802.11aiFast Initial Link Setup2016ManagementReduces initial association time via FILS Authentication. Pre-associated state caching. Target: <100 ms.Important for high-density venues where many devices associate simultaneously.
802.11-2016 (TGmc)Maintenance Roll-up2016Roll-upAdds ac/ad/ae/aa/af. TGmc also defined FTM (Fine Timing Measurement) - industry calls it "802.11mc FTM" (basis of Android Wi-Fi RTT).FTM origin - precursor of 802.11az.
802.11akGeneral Links (GLK)2018BridgingEnables 802.11 links to act as transit links inside 802.1Q bridged networks (per-VLAN, per-TID transit).Targeted at industrial / bridged topologies. Rare in enterprise WLAN.
802.11aqPre-Association Discovery (PAD)2018ManagementService discovery before association (extends 802.11u/ANQP): discover services (printing, media) advertised by network without associating.Complements Hotspot 2.0 / Passpoint. Limited client adoption.
802.11ajChina Millimeter Wave (CMMW)2018PHYAdaptation of 802.11ad for China 59-64 GHz and 45 GHz bands.China-specific; rarely encountered elsewhere.
802.11-2020 (TGmd)Maintenance Roll-up2020Roll-upAdds ah/ai/aj/ak/aq. Current most-cited baseline document.Most clause references in this workbook use 802.11-2020 numbering.
802.11axHE / Wi-Fi 6/6E2021PHYOFDMA DL/UL, MU-MIMO UL, BSS Color, TWT, 4× longer symbols. Wi-Fi 6E extends to 6 GHz. Up to 9.6 Gbps.Most efficient standard yet. Major improvement for dense deployments.
802.11ayWiGig 2.0 / 60 GHz2021PHYExtends 802.11ad: channel bonding (4× 2.16 GHz), MIMO, up to ~100 Gbps. Adds outdoor backhauling.Enables 60 GHz backhaul for 5G small cells and stadium connectivity.
802.11baWake-up Radio2021MACDefines an ultra-low-power (~1 µW) wake-up receiver that listens continuously, waking the main radio only when needed.Extends battery life for IoT devices to potentially 10+ years.
802.11azNext-Gen Positioning2022ManagementDefines fine timing measurement (FTM) improvements for sub-meter indoor positioning. Uses HE NDP-based ranging.Sub-10 cm ranging possible. Key for RTLS, asset tracking, indoor navigation.
802.11bdNext-Gen V2X2022V2XSuccessor to 802.11p: LDPC, midambles for Doppler, 10/20 MHz in 5.9 GHz ITS band, optional 60 GHz. ~2x range and 2x throughput vs 11p.Competes/coexists with 3GPP C-V2X. Backward compatible with 802.11p.
802.11bcEnhanced Broadcast Services (eBCS)2023MACBroadcast of authenticated content to non-associated STAs (one-to-many, no association required), with signed frames.Use cases: venue info push, emergency broadcast. Early adoption stage.
802.11bbLight Communication (LiFi)2023PHYAdds light (infrared 800-1000 nm) as PHY medium, reusing 802.11 MAC. ~9.6 Gbps class rates.EMI-free niches: hospitals, aircraft, secure rooms. Very limited ecosystem.
802.11beEHT / Wi-Fi 72024 (approved); 2025 (published)PHY320 MHz channels, 4096-QAM, Multi-Link Operation, Preamble Puncturing, 8×8 MIMO, R-TWT. Up to ~23 Gbps (8 SS).Targets <1 ms latency deterministic links. Ratified 2024.
802.11bfWLAN Sensing2024/2025SensingStandardized Wi-Fi sensing: SENS procedures reusing PHY (CSI measurement) for presence/gesture/vital-sign detection. Works 2.4/5/6 GHz + 60 GHz.Wi-Fi CERTIFIED sensing programs emerging. Privacy discussions ongoing.
802.11bhDevice Identification with Randomized MAC2024/2025Privacy/MgmtRestores network-side device identity (device ID, IRM - Identifiable Random MAC) that randomized MAC addresses broke, without exposing global MAC.Solves DHCP reservation / captive portal / analytics breakage caused by MAC randomization.
802.11-2024 / REVme (TGme)Maintenance Roll-up2024/2025Roll-upConsolidates ax/ay/az/ba/bb/bc/bd + corrigenda into new baseline (published as IEEE 802.11-2024).New baseline; clause numbers shift again vs -2020.
802.11bnUHR / Wi-Fi 8Draft (target ~2028)PHYUltra-High Reliability. Enhanced MLO, coordinated OFDMA, NSTR (non-simultaneous TX/RX) improvements. Target: 100 Gbps.In TGbn task group. Focus on reliability and latency vs raw speed.
802.11bk320 MHz PositioningIn progressPositioningExtends 802.11az FTM ranging to 320 MHz EHT channels for higher accuracy (larger BW = finer time resolution).Pairs with Wi-Fi 7. Sub-10 cm class accuracy targets.
802.11biEnhanced Service with Data PrivacyIn progress (~2026)PrivacyClient privacy enhancements: encrypting more identifying fields, protecting pre-association exchanges, mitigating tracking via PHY/MAC fingerprinting.TGbi active. Watch for changes to probe/assoc contents.
802.11TWireless Performance Prediction (WPP)Never completedTestTest methodology and metrics recommended practice for predicting Wi-Fi performance.WITHDRAWN. Capital T = recommended practice. Test methods live on in Wi-Fi Alliance and vendor test plans.
