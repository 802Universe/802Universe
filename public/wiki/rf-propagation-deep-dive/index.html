Rayleigh Fading (NLOS)PDF: f(r) = (r/σ²)·exp(-r²/2σ²) K-factor = 0 (no LOS)Amplitude distribution when no dominant LOS component. Multiple reflections with similar power combine randomly. Envelope follows Rayleigh distribution.NLOS indoor environments. Average of K=0 in TGn model A/B/C/E/F. Worst-case fading depth = -20 to -40 dB below mean. Affects cell-edge STAs most.Proakis 5th Ed. Ch.14 IEEE 802.11 TGn
Rician Fading (LOS)PDF: f(r) = (r/σ²)·exp(-(r²+A²)/2σ²)·I₀(Ar/σ²) K = A²/2σ² (K-factor)Amplitude distribution with dominant LOS component (amplitude A) plus scattered components (variance σ²). K-factor = ratio of LOS power to scatter power.LOS in offices (TGn Model D: K=3 dB). Higher K = less fading = more reliable link. K→∞ = no fading (pure LOS). K=0 = Rayleigh.Proakis 5th Ed. Ch.14 IEEE 802.11 TGax eval
Coherence BandwidthBc ≈ 1/(5·στ) (90% coherence) Bc ≈ 1/(2π·στ) (50% coherence)Frequency range over which channel response is approximately constant (correlated). Above Bc: channel varies across frequency = frequency-selective fading.OFDM subcarrier spacing must be << Bc for flat fading per subcarrier. 802.11ax: 78.125 kHz spacing. Indoor office στ=30-50 ns → Bc=4-6 MHz >> 78.125 kHz ✓ Outdoor στ=100-300 ns → Bc=0.6-2 MHz >> 78.125 kHz ✓ITU-R M.2135 Proakis Ch.14
Coherence TimeTc = 0.423/fD where fD = v·fc/c (Doppler) Example: v=3 km/h, fc=5 GHz: fD = 0.83 Hz×5GHz/c ≈ 13.9 Hz Tc ≈ 30 msTime over which channel impulse response is approximately constant. Above Tc: channel changes during packet = time-selective fading.802.11ax symbol = 16 µs. At walking speed (3 km/h), Tc ≈ 30 ms >> 16 µs → channel stable per symbol ✓ At vehicle speed (100 km/h): Tc ≈ 0.9 ms - channel may change during 64-symbol PPDU. 802.11bd (V2X) must handle this.ITU-R M.2135 Proakis Ch.14
Doppler SpreadfD_max = v·fc/c ICI threshold: Δf << 1/TcMaximum frequency shift due to motion. Creates ICI (Inter-Carrier Interference) in OFDM when subcarrier spacing is comparable to Doppler spread.802.11ax: Δf = 78.125 kHz. fD at 100 km/h @ 5 GHz = 463 Hz. ICI ratio = 463/78125 = 0.006 = negligible. 802.11ah (sub-1 GHz): Δf = 31.25 kHz. Even lower Doppler spread due to lower fc.IEEE 802.11ax D8.0 §27.3 ETSI TR 103 594
RMS Delay Spread (στ)στ = √(Σ(τk²·Pk)/ΣPk - (Στk·Pk/ΣPk)²) where Pk = power of path k, τk = delayRoot Mean Square of the power-weighted delay of all multipath components. Key parameter for OFDM guard interval design.CP must be ≥ στ to prevent ISI. 802.11ax CP options: 0.8/1.6/3.2 µs: • 0.8 µs: indoor office (στ < 100 ns) ✓ • 1.6 µs: large office/warehouse ✓ • 3.2 µs: outdoor/stadium (στ < 500 ns) ✓IEEE 802.11 TGn channel models ITU-R P.1238
Fresnel Zone Clearancer₁ = √(λd₁d₂/d) 60% clearance needed to avoid diffraction loss Example: 802.11ac at 5 GHz, d=100m: r₁ = √(0.06×50×50/100) = 1.7mEllipsoidal region around LOS path where reflected waves can constructively/destructively interfere. Obstruction of 1st Fresnel zone → significant diffraction loss.Critical for outdoor point-to-point Wi-Fi links. 5 GHz at 100m needs 1.7m clearance above obstacles. At 2.4 GHz: 2.5m clearance. Taller obstacles or longer links = larger Fresnel zone.ITU-R P.530 IEEE 802.11 outdoor deployment guides
Two-Ray Ground ReflectionPL = 40log(d) - 20log(hT) - 20log(hR) Breakpoint: d_bp = 4·hT·hR/λBeyond breakpoint, both direct and ground-reflected rays combine. PL exponent increases from 2 to 4. Destructive interference at breakpoint.Outdoor Wi-Fi: flat ground, AP at 3m, client at 1.5m, 5 GHz → d_bp = 4×3×1.5/0.06 = 300m. Beyond 300m: n=4. Important for outdoor campus/park coverage planning.ITU-R P.1411 IEEE 802.11ah (long-range)
MIMO Channel RankH = U·Σ·V† Rank = number of non-zero singular values Capacity = Σ log₂(1 + λᵢ·SNR/Nt)MIMO channel matrix H (Nr×Nt). SVD decomposition reveals independent spatial channels (eigenmodes). Rank = number of useful streams.Rank < min(Nt,Nr) if paths are highly correlated (few independent scatterers). Corridor LOS: rank often = 1 (no MIMO gain). Rich scattering NLOS: rank ≈ min(Nt,Nr). Use cross-pol antennas + scattering to improve rank.Foschini & Gans 1998 Telatar 1999 IEEE 802.11 TGn
PAPR of OFDMPAPR = max|s(t)|² / E[|s(t)|²] Theoretical max: 10·log₁₀(N) dB 802.11ax 20 MHz: 10·log₁₀(256) = 24 dB Typical after clipping: 8-13 dBPeak-to-Average Power Ratio. OFDM has high PAPR because N subcarriers can add constructively at peak. Power amplifier must handle peak without saturation (clipping = distortion = EVM degradation).6 dB IBO (Input Back-Off) typical. Limits effective TX power. DPD (Digital Pre-Distortion) reduces required IBO to 3 dB. Higher-order QAM (4096-QAM) more sensitive to PAPR clipping.IEEE 802.11-2020 §27.3.14 ETSI EN 301 893 §4.2.3
